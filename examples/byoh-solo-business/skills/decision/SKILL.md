# Decision — Justify by Business Impact, Name the Opportunity Cost

A technical choice is defensible only when you can say what it does to revenue,
users, or market position — and what you give up to do it.

## Process

1. **State the business lever.** Which of {revenue, users, market position,
   cost, risk} does this move, and in which direction?
2. **Quantify within a horizon.** "Improves retention by X% within 30 days" —
   not "is better".
3. **Name the opportunity cost.** What is the next-best alternative you are
   *not* doing?
4. **State the reversal cost.** Can this be undone cheaply? A reversible choice
   needs less evidence than an irreversible one.

## Anti-Rationalization

- "It's a best practice" → best practices optimize for someone else's business;
   name *your* lever.
- "It's more scalable" → only counts if scale is your current constraint.
- "We'll figure out the cost later" → the cost *is* the decision; decide it now.

## Evidence

- A decision is well-framed when it names the lever, the quantified effect, the
  opportunity cost, and the reversal cost.

## Red Flags

- Pure technical elegance as the justification.
- No opportunity cost named (the default alternative is never "nothing").
- "Irreversible" choices made on weak evidence.
