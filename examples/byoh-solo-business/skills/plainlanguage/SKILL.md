# Plain Language — Lead with the Outcome, Kill the Jargon

If the reader can't act on it, the message failed. Lead with what they get;
explain how only if asked.

## Process

1. **Lead with the outcome.** First sentence: what changes for the reader.
2. **Replace jargon with plain words.** "Deprecate" → "we're removing";
   "leverage" → "use". Keep a term only when the audience genuinely shares it.
3. **One idea per sentence.** Compound sentences hide the verb that matters.
4. **Read it aloud.** If you stumble, the reader will.

## Anti-Rationalization

- "It's precise" → jargon isn't precision, it's exclusion. Precision is in the
  numbers and the verbs.
- "Stakeholders know these terms" → name *which* stakeholders. The CFO and the
  customer are different audiences.
- "It has to be formal" → formality is tone, not opacity.

## Evidence

- A message works when a non-expert can state, in their own words, what to do
  next after reading it once.

## Red Flags

- The first sentence is background, not outcome.
- Acronyms undefined on first use.
- A paragraph where the action is buried in the middle.
